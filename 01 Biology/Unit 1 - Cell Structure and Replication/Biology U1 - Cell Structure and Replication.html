Meiosis notes — a scaffold for the homework

Headings and prompts only — the writing is yours, in your own words

1What meiosis is and what it makes

  • Define meiosis in one sentence. Which kind of reproduction is it used for?
  • What does it produce? Name both human gametes.
  • How many cells come out of one parent cell, and are they identical to each other?

2The halving, and why it matters

  • Diploid and haploid — define both, and say which is the body cell and which is the gamete.
  • Human numbers: body cell ___ chromosomes, gamete ___ chromosomes, zygote ___ chromosomes.
  • Finish this sentence: if the number did not halve, then every generation...

3Where the variation comes from

  • Random assortment — what gets shared out randomly?
  • Crossing over — what is exchanged, and between which chromosomes? Draw it.
  • Random fusion of gametes — why does it matter that any sperm can fertilise any egg?

4Mitosis vs meiosis

  • Draw the table: number of divisions, cells produced, identical or different, chromosome number, what it is used for.
  • One sentence on what the two processes have in common.
  • One sentence on the single biggest difference.

Lab & exam toolkit

The command terms Ms. Luna uses, and what a full Criterion B report needs

✎Command terms in science

  • State — give a specific name, value or brief answer. No explanation, no working.
  • Outline — give a brief account or summary.
  • Describe — give a detailed account of a situation, event, pattern or process.
  • Apply — use an idea, equation, principle or theory in a given situation.
  • Suggest — propose a solution, hypothesis or other possible answer.
  • Read the command term first and answer at that depth — writing a paragraph for “state” wins no marks, and one line for “describe” loses them.

BCriterion B report checklist

  • Research question — names both the independent and the dependent variable, and is specific enough that someone else could design the same investigation.
  • Variables — independent variable with 5 conditions; dependent variable with the measuring device and its units; 2 or more control variables, each with how you control it.
  • Hypothesis — a testable prediction of the effect of the IV on the DV, justified with scientific reasoning.
  • Materials — a point-form list with sizes and quantities specified.
  • Method — a numbered list of direct instructions, covering all 5 conditions and at least 3 trials, stating that a mean will be calculated.
  • Safety — more than 2 hazards, each with its preventative measure and action plan.
