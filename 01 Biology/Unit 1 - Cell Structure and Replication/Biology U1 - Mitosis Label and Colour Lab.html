Mitosis: label & colour lab

The worksheet asks you to label seven structures across the five stages. Do it here first — tap a label, then tap the slot you think it belongs to — and you will know the answers cold before you put pen to paper.

Step 1 — the colour key

Use the same colour for the same structure in every cell. That consistency is the whole point of the exercise: it makes the changes between the stages jump out.

Step 2 — label every stage

Tap a label above, then tap a numbered slot. Tap a filled slot to empty it again.

Step 3 — get the order right

Tap the five stages in the order they happen.

1. ___   2. ___   3. ___   4. ___   5. ___

Why the numbers change from stage to stage. Not all seven structures are visible in every cell, and that is the biology, not a mistake in the sheet. The nuclear membrane and the nucleolus break down during prophase and only come back in telophase. The spindle does not exist until prophase builds it. And what you call number 5 changes too: loose chromatin in interphase, condensed chromosomes from prophase on, separated chromatids once anaphase pulls them apart.

One sentence per stage is what the annotation task wants — each stage card above gives you the version to write, in your own words.
